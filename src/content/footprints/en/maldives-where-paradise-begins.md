What's the strangest way you can imagine celebrating Christmas? I usually imagine Christmas as time with my family, warm hugs and cosy clothes, hot drinks and lots of snow. But this Christmas was different – I spent it on a Maldivian island, Ukulhas, with my man.

You've probably seen lots of celebrity photos from the Maldives. There are two types of islands there – resort and non-resort islands.

We chose a non-resort island, far from all the fancy stuff and a great escape from the daily routine. Have you ever dreamed of running away from your normal life and finding yourself on a lonely island? Ukulhas is your place.

## Weather

Our journey started at the end of December, and we spent New Year's Eve there. We survived a temperature swing of more than 30 °C – twice. Feeling that difference is really extreme, so if it's winter where you live, be ready. You feel it most when you return from the sun and heat to the snow. Unfortunately, the warm sun rays don't last long.

## Reaching your island

How exactly do you reach one of the Maldivian islands? First, you fly to Malé, the capital of the Maldives.

Then you have 3 options – a public ferry, a speedboat or a seaplane transfer.

- **The public ferry** is cheap ($5 per person each way) but very slow.
- **The seaplane transfer** sounds fancy, but it's too expensive ($360 per adult, round trip), and I don't think it's worth the money. I'd rather spend it on diving.

So we chose **the speedboat** ($50 per person each way). The speedboat ride can be slow and sleepy, especially after 10 hours of travelling. But if the waves are crazy... oh, you have to hold on tight and keep away from the water that splashes into the cabin with every second wave.

## Finally there

When we finally arrived on Ukulhas, a very kind guy from our hotel, New Moon Village – Saiful – met us and walked us to the hotel, just 5 minutes away. The whole island is 1 km long and 0.2 km wide, so you can reach any place on it in less than 10 minutes. If the most important thing for you on a seaside holiday is how close you are to the beach – on Ukulhas, wherever you are, you're close to the beach. Everything is close. For 10 days, the island felt like our own neighbourhood.

New Moon Village is a very nice place and one of the cheapest on the island. This is what our room looked like when we arrived – how could I not fall in love at first sight?

![Our room at New Moon Village](/blog/xiblog-maldives-6f445ba5.jpg)

The staff are awesome – they help with everything you need, bring you an umbrella and sunbeds, and find you the best offers for excursions and diving. The rooms are always clean, and Saiful makes fresh, cold young coconuts for the hot beach days. Every morning we could choose from 4 breakfasts – 2 typical Maldivian and 2 typical American. (More about the food in the [Savour section](/en/savour/maldives-food--drinks-guide/).)

## The beach

I was desperate to see the beach after this long, long journey. But as you walk through the island, the beach is invisible – you're walking through a small village with little houses, lots of children playing and no sign of the ocean. But then you see some palm trees and greenery, and when you walk further in, through a kind of natural tunnel, there it is – the most beautiful beach I have ever seen. There's a kind of border – between the ordinary and the extraordinary, between normal daily life and paradise, between people and nature, between the dusty streets and the turquoise sea.
