Livigno is a town and a special-administration territory in the province of Sondrio, in the Lombardy region of Italy. It lies in the Italian Alps, right next to the Swiss border.

It is small, cosy and surrounded by snow, sitting at the foot of two huge snowy mountains – Mottolino and Carosello 3000. It has the typical atmosphere of a Christmas movie: the warmth of the fireplace inside, fluffy snow outside, and everything around you white, pure and immaculate. Well, this is Livigno. Unfortunately, we weren't there for Christmas, but we spent two special holidays in this magnificent place, relaxing and escaping the grey, boring everyday reality.

## How to get to Livigno?

### *By car*

The journey is long and almost impossible to do in one go. With several drivers in the car it's much more manageable. The distance from Sofia is nearly 1,500 km. Of course, the long and perhaps exhausting journey has its advantages – beautiful views, snow-covered hills and coffee breaks with Italian coffee ☕

We also visited Ljubljana, the capital of Slovenia, where we spent the night to break up the trip. There's plenty to see in the dragon city with its bridges over the river Ljubljanica. Every city with a river running through it is somehow exciting and invigorating – the whole architecture evokes fairy tales and mystique. We visited Ljubljana Castle, perhaps the most famous landmark in the city. It rises on a high hill in the centre and can be seen from afar.

### *By plane*

There's no direct flight to Livigno, but there are several options: a flight from Sofia to Milan (Ryanair, Wizz Air, Lufthansa) and then a bus, or a flight from Sofia to Zurich and then a train (as I mentioned, Livigno is right on the border between Italy and Switzerland). Flights aren't expensive, and many airlines let you take ski equipment.

## The Munt la Schera tunnel

After Ljubljana, we continued our journey to the final destination.

The Munt la Schera tunnel is a must-know if you travel by car. At 3,394 metres long, it connects the Engadine valley via the Swiss town of Zernez with Lake Livigno, below the Munt la Schera peak. In winter, it's the only way to reach the resort. The tunnel is single-lane – just wide enough for one vehicle – so the direction of traffic changes at set hours: from 6 to 9 in the morning it's open towards Switzerland (leaving Livigno), and from 10 to 18 towards Italy (into Livigno). So you can enter the resort through the tunnel at 10 am at the earliest, and if you want to leave after a ski day, plan your departure for after 6 pm.

## Livigno

And here is Livigno itself. We arrived in the evening, at around 6. In front of us was the pretty town, buried in snow, with small glowing cottages – like a winter fairy tale.

![Livigno at night](/blog/xiblog-livigno-c7ccfe6c.jpg)

We stayed at the Intermonti Hotel, which is a bit far from the centre, but getting around is easy: free buses run all over town – to the centre, the hotels and the lifts.

## The slopes

And now to the slopes, for all you avid skiers.

![Ski slopes in Livigno](/blog/xiblog-livigno-592a6e92.jpg)

There are two separate ski areas in Livigno – Mottolino and Carosello 3000 – on two different mountains. In my opinion, the runs on Mottolino are steeper and more extreme, while those on Carosello are longer and gentler, very wide, with amazing views of exceptional nature. A gondola takes you up to both peaks. And here I can't skip the scariest story of my stay in Livigno – the swinging cabins.

![The lifts in Livigno](/blog/xiblog-livigno-e7c1d815.jpg)

One day (we were in Livigno for a week), all the lifts were stopped because of strong wind.

Thirsty for the slopes after a few Aperol walks and some shopping in the town's duty-free zone, we were ready to get back on the runs the next day.

*(Situated on the border between Italy and Switzerland, Livigno is a duty-free zone and the best winter resort for shopping. You can find lots of brands at normal prices – and don't forget to buy Italian coffee and Swiss chocolate 🤤)*

And... back to the slopes.

First, we headed to Carosello with the cute yellow gondola. But after one ride up, the lift was stopped because of the wind. Then we moved to Mottolino, hoping the conditions there would be better. We went up to the top, and on the last ascent we were on an open chairlift. Apart from the terrible wind blowing right through us, the chairlift swayed from side to side. The technicians had to stop it for a while, wait for the wind to calm down and then start it again. The problem was that the wind never calmed down. We were hanging metres above the ground, in the open, just praying that our four-seater wouldn't fall. I've never been afraid of heights or lifts, and never paranoid about falling. Except this time. All I wanted was to feel the ground beneath my feet. After about half an hour in the air, we finally made it. We headed to the lower part of Mottolino, where there was a closed cabin lift. To our surprise, once we got on, it was packed and the whole cabin swung indescribably. People started screaming 😱 And on top of that, we saw a technician in front of us climbing up the lift pylon.
